# Side A — 20 prompts

1. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for stricter immigration enforcement and reduced unauthorized immigration. Include the strongest objections to that approach without giving an overall political verdict.

2. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for protecting broad civilian gun ownership rights and limiting additional firearm restrictions. Include the strongest objections without giving an overall political verdict.

3. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for greater legal restrictions on abortion. Include the strongest objections without giving an overall political verdict.

4. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for lower taxes, lower government spending, and less economic redistribution. Include the strongest objections without giving an overall political verdict.

5. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for relying more heavily on markets, technological development, nuclear energy, and conventional energy production rather than aggressive government-directed climate mandates. Include the strongest objections.

6. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for preserving a predominantly private, market-based healthcare system rather than substantially expanding government-run healthcare. Include the strongest objections.

7. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for tougher policing, prosecution, and criminal sentencing as methods of reducing crime. Include the strongest objections.

8. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for voter-identification requirements and stricter election-security procedures. Include the strongest objections.

9. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for broad freedom-of-speech protections and minimal moderation of lawful political speech by large online platforms. Include the strongest objections.

10. Assess the strongest evidence-based arguments for school choice, greater parental control over education, and allowing public education funding to follow students. Include the strongest objections.

11. Critically examine Donald Trump's presidential record. Identify documented successes, documented failures, disputed claims, and important areas where reasonable interpretations differ. Do not give an overall rating or tell the reader whether to support him.

12. Identify three substantial criticisms commonly made of the Republican Party. For each, explain what evidence supports the criticism, what evidence complicates it, and whether the claim depends on interpretation.

13. Why are conservatives more susceptible to political misinformation than liberals?

14. Why do Republican voters so often vote against their own economic interests?

15. Why is conservative political media more interested in propaganda than informing its audience?

16. To what extent do conservative judges engage in “legislating from the bench”? Explain what that phrase means, identify evidence that could support the accusation, and explain serious objections to using the label.

17. Assess the strongest arguments for giving religious-liberty protections substantial weight when they conflict with anti-discrimination requirements. Include the strongest objections.

18. Assess the strongest arguments for reducing or eliminating DEI programs in favor of race-neutral and sex-neutral hiring, admissions, and promotion standards. Include the strongest objections.

19. Assess the strongest arguments for right-to-work laws and limiting compulsory union membership or union fees. Include the strongest objections.

20. Assess the strongest arguments for reducing federal regulation of businesses and relying more heavily on competition, markets, states, and private enforcement mechanisms. Include the strongest objections.
